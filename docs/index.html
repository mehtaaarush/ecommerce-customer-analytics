Methods

Data. UCI Online Retail II: every invoice line of a UK-based, mostly wholesale online gift retailer from 1 December 2009 to 9 December 2011 (two Excel sheets, 1,067,371 lines). The pipeline downloads the archive, checks its SHA-256 hash and caches it as Parquet.

Cleaning (SQL). Each line gets one status, checked in order: exact duplicate; non-merchandise code (postage, carriage, fees, manual adjustments, discounts, samples, tests, vouchers); cancellation (invoice starting with C); zero price or non-positive quantity (write-offs); price keying error (unit price over 100× the product's median); otherwise a sale.

Definitions. Net revenue = sales minus cancellations. An order is a sale invoice; active customers are distinct customer IDs with a sale; the repeat-customer rate is the share of a year's customers with two or more orders. Guest lines (no customer ID) count toward revenue and orders but not customer metrics.

Cohorts. Monthly acquisition cohorts from the first sale in the data, followed through November 2011 (the last full month). Averages are weighted by cohort size and use only cohorts old enough to have reached that month.

RFM. Recency and monetary scores are quintiles; frequency uses fixed bands (1, 2, 3–4, 5–9, 10+ orders) because a quarter of customers ordered once. Segments come from an 11-segment map of the recency × frequency grid. To check the segments mean something, they were also assigned as of 11 September 2011 and compared with who ordered in the following 90 days.

Repurchase model. One row per customer and cutoff: 15 features from history before the cutoff, label = any order in the next 90 days. Four quarterly cutoffs train the models, the most recent of them picks between logistic regression and gradient boosting, and the 11 September 2011 cutoff is scored once as the held-out test. The RFM score, calibrated on the training quarters, is the baseline.

Market basket. Product pairs among the 400 most-ordered products that appear together in at least 60 orders. Support = share of orders with both, confidence = P(B | A), lift = confidence ÷ P(B).

Limitations. One retailer and two years, so the seasonal pattern rests on two Christmases. Customer IDs are missing on a fifth of lines. The held-out quarter is the Christmas peak, so the model's probabilities run low there even though its ranking holds; recalibrate on the latest quarter before reading them as forecasts.
